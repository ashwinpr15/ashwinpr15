# Deliverable 5 — Admissions, Visa & Funding Master Timeline

<div class="meta">Start date: 15 October 2026 · Target: arrive in Ireland around 25 August 2027 · Dates marked <strong>(official)</strong> come from a published source. All other dates are recommended operating targets that leave a safety buffer.</div>

## Phase 0 — Launch (15 – 31 October 2026)

| By | Action | Detail / tactic |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Oct | **Book IELTS Academic** (IDP or British Council, Bengaluru) | Test date in the **first half of November**. Target 7.0 overall, no band below 6.5; the minimum is 6.5 / 6.0. Results arrive in 3–5 days (computer-delivered), which leaves a retake slot in December if needed. |
| 20 Oct | Order documents | Bangalore University (B.Com) and VTU/DSCE (MBA): **official transcripts for all semesters, degree certificates, and a CGPA-to-percentage conversion letter**. Allow 2–4 weeks. |
| 22 Oct | Get the B.Com syllabus | Extract the Financial Accounting, Management Accounting and Business Finance syllabi, which Galway's Pathway A prerequisite requires. |
| 25 Oct | **Brief three referees** | Send each one the CV, SOP and their draft letter from Deliverable 4. Confirm their **official email addresses**, because the portals will contact them directly. |
| 25 Oct | Experience proof | EY relieving/experience letter, Amazon employment verification letter, recent payslips |
| 28 Oct | Create portal accounts | Galway (open since 1 Oct), UL, DCU, Trinity Business School. Download the checklist for each one. |
| 31 Oct | Register for **Education in Ireland** fair / webinars | Use the fair for **application-fee waiver codes** and to speak to the Galway, UL and DCU representatives. Use the email script in Deliverable 1 §6. |
| 31 Oct | **Funding reality check with family** | Confirm the savings figure (assumed ₹5–8 L), possible co-applicant (parent with ITRs) and any collateral. This decides whether Dublin is realistic. |

## Phase 2 — Offers, deposits & GOI-IES (January – March 2027)

| By | Action | Detail / tactic |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | **Receive offers** (conditional or unconditional) | Typical turnaround is 2–8 weeks. Record each offer's conditions, deposit amount and deadline, refund policy if the visa is refused, and the confirmed 2027/28 fee. |
| Jan | **Offer comparison** | Use the decision rule in Deliverable 1 §4.3: **Galway if any scholarship or ₹4 L+ extra support; otherwise UL; Dublin only with GOI-IES or ₹10 L+ extra.** |
| **Late Jan** (expected) | **GOI-IES 2027 call opens** | The 2026 call opened on 29 Jan 2026 *(official)*. The applicant **must already hold an offer**. The application covers academic record, a personal statement, reference(s) and a statement of how she will contribute to Ireland–India links. |
| Feb | **Pay one deposit only** | Usually €500–€1,500 (₹55k–₹1.65 L). Before paying, check (a) the **refund-on-visa-refusal** clause in writing and (b) that the deposit counts toward the €6,000 tuition minimum for the visa. Do not pay deposits at two universities. |
| Feb | Meet offer conditions | Upload the final IELTS score and any outstanding documents. Aim to turn a conditional offer into an **unconditional (final) offer** by mid-March, because the loan and visa both need it. |
| **~12 Mar** (expected; the 2026 deadline was **12 Mar 2026** *(official)*) | **Submit GOI-IES** | Results came out in early June in the 2026 cycle. Continue the full self-funded plan in parallel; never wait for this result. |
| Mar | University accommodation | Apply on day one of the residence portal (Galway: Corrib Village / Goldcrest; UL: on-campus villages). Places are limited and often allocated early. |

## Phase 3 — Loan sanction & proof of funds (March – May 2027)

| By | Action | Detail / tactic |
|---|---|---|
| 15 Mar | **Formal loan applications** to 2–3 lenders | Submit the final offer letter, fee structure, IELTS, academic documents, CV, co-applicant KYC/ITRs/bank statements and collateral papers if needed. |
| 10 Apr | **Compare written sanction letters** | Compare effective interest rate, processing fee, insurance add-ons, moratorium (course + 6–12 months), whether interest can be serviced during study, prepayment charges, and the **tuition vs living-cost disbursement split**. Get everything **in writing**. |
| 20 Apr | Sign sanction; **disburse tuition** | The lender pays the university directly (wire to the university's Irish bank or via its approved payment portal). **Pay at least €6,000; ideally the full first-year fee.** Keep the **university's fee receipt**, which is mandatory for the visa. |
| Apr | TCS check | Confirm that the bank applies the **nil-TCS treatment for loan-financed education remittances**. Get a Form 15CA/CB or remittance certificate for the records. |
| May | **Build the living-cost evidence** | The visa needs **immediate access to at least €10,000 (≈₹11 L)**. Provide (a) the loan sanction letter showing the living-cost component, **and** (b) a bank statement in her or her sponsor's name showing ₹11 L+ in liquid funds. Keep the balance **stable for 6 months**: no unexplained large deposits before filing. |
| May | Sponsor pack (if parents contribute) | Sponsor's letter of support, ITRs for 3 years, 6-month bank statements, proof of relationship, and an affidavit of support. |
| May | Medical insurance | Buy an Irish-compliant private student health policy (≈€200–€550 a year) covering the full study period. |

## Phase 4 — Visa, accommodation & pre-departure (late May – July 2027)

**Visa rule:** an Irish Long Stay 'D' Study Visa application can be lodged **up to 3 months before travel**. Processing is **about 8 weeks** *(official guidance)* and longer in peak season. For a ~25 August arrival, submit **between 25 May and 15 June 2027**.

| By | Action | Detail / tactic |
|---|---|---|
| 25 May | Complete the **AVATS online application** | Long Stay 'D' — Study. The single-entry visa fee is €60, paid at VFS in INR, plus the VFS service charge. |
| 25 May – 15 Jun | **VFS Global, Bengaluru**: biometrics and documents | Book the appointment early in the window. |
| — | **Visa document pack** | ☐ Passport + old passports · ☐ AVATS summary signed · ☐ 2 photos · ☐ **Letter of acceptance** (unconditional) · ☐ **Fee-payment receipt** (≥ €6,000; ideally the full fee) · ☐ **Evidence of €10,000+ access** + loan sanction · ☐ 6-month bank statements · ☐ Sponsor pack (if any) · ☐ IELTS TRF · ☐ Degree certificates and transcripts · ☐ **Cover letter / study plan** (summarise the SOP's "why specialise after an MBA" and the commitment to comply with immigration conditions) · ☐ **Gap explanation** for Oct 2023 – Jul 2024 · ☐ Employment letters (Amazon, EY) · ☐ CV · ☐ Medical insurance · ☐ Fully translated/attested documents where required |
| Jun | **Book accommodation** | Don't wait for the visa decision; choose a contract with a **visa-refusal cancellation clause**. Galway: Corrib Village, Goldcrest, Westside or Newcastle shared houses. Limerick: UL villages or Castletroy/Dooradoyle rentals. Use Daft.ie and the university's official housing portal **only**, and never send a deposit for a room you have not verified (rental scams are common). |
| Jun | Resign / notice planning | Work out the Amazon notice period and set a last working day around 31 July. Ask for the experience letter and relieving letter. |
| Jun | If the visa is refused | Ask for the refusal letter. An **appeal** is possible within **2 months**, so address each stated reason point by point. Check the university's fee-refund terms. |
| Jul | **Visa decision**, then book flights | Bengaluru → Dublin (Shannon for Limerick). Book once the visa is in the passport. |
| Jul | Forex | A forex card or international debit card with about €1,500 for the first month, plus the living-cost loan disbursement planned for September. |

## Phase 5 — Arrival & first 60 days (August – October 2027)

| When | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ~25 Aug | Fly to Ireland | Arrive 7–10 days before orientation. Carry originals of every visa document in hand luggage for border control. |
| Week 1 | Open a bank account | AIB / Bank of Ireland / PTSB, using a university enrolment letter and proof of address. Revolut works as a bridge. |
| Week 1–2 | **Register for the IRP** (Stamp 2) | Book through the immigration service as directed for the region (≈ €300). The IRP card is what allows her to work. |
| Week 2–4 | **PPSN** | Apply once she has a job offer, through MyWelfare.ie. |
| Sept | Orientation and careers service | Register with the careers service in week 1. Big 4 and Tech EMEA **graduate programmes open in September–October and close early**, so apply in the first term. |
| Sept–Oct | Part-time job | 10–15 hrs/week is a sensible cap for a 90-ECTS one-year programme. Aim for roles that add to the CV (finance or admin assistant, bookkeeping, university jobs) where possible. |

## Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Galway prerequisite query (accounting modules) | Low | Upload the syllabus extract with the application |
| IELTS below 6.5 in one band | Medium | Book early; keep the December retake slot |
| 2027/28 fees rise | High | 4% contingency already in the model |
| No scholarship | High | The base plan never assumes one; UL stays fully viable |
| Loan sanction below ₹30 L | Medium | Apply to three lenders; UL needs the least |
| Accommodation shortage | High in Galway and Dublin | Apply for residences in March; private search from June; scam check |
| Visa delay | Medium | File in late May; complete pack; gap explanation included |
| Part-time job slow to materialise | Medium | Not part of the base plan; the 1-month cash buffer covers it |
