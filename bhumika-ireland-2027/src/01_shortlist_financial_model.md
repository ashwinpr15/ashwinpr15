## 1. Why this shortlist (and why not the IDP list)

The IDP shortlist dated 17 Sept 2026 lists 17 UK *International Business / Management* MSc programmes. Every one is rejected for three reasons:

1. **Duplication of the MBA.** A second broad management degree adds little academic value. It is also the weakest story for a visa officer, who will ask why an MBA holder with 3.5 years of experience is repeating general management. A specialist degree gives a clean answer.
2. **Weaker post-study route.** UK Graduate Route applications made on or after 1 January 2027 give 18 months, not 2 years. Ireland gives Level 9 graduates up to 24 months on Stamp 1G.
3. **Price.** Most of the IDP programmes cost £18,000–£32,000 before London or UK living costs, and none of them builds on her tax or compliance experience.

**The anchor narrative used across every document:**

| Existing asset | Becomes | Target programme |
|---|---|---|
| B.Com (8.43 CGPA) + EY Global Mobility Tax | International accounting, reporting, audit and analytics | Galway MSc IAA · DCU MSc IAB |
| Amazon GSRC compliance audits, RCA, CAPA | Operational risk, regulatory compliance, financial regulation | UL MSc Insurance & Risk Management |
| MBA (general management) | The management base. The new master's adds technical depth on top of it and does not repeat it | All |

### 4.4 Immigration proof of funds (the visa view, not the real budget)

For a one-year course, Irish Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) expects:

* **At least €6,000 of tuition already paid** to the university, or the full fee if it is under €6,000. Most students pay the full first-year fee before applying, which strengthens the application.
* **Immediate access to at least €10,000 for living expenses**, plus evidence that any unpaid balance of fees is covered.

| Programme | Fees + €10,000 | ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| Galway | €30,890 | ₹33.98 L |
| UL | €28,400 | ₹31.24 L |
| DCU (award) | €31,000 | ₹34.10 L |

The loan (₹30 L) plus savings (₹6.5 L) covers the **visa threshold** for every Core programme. The visa threshold is a minimum and is **not** the real cost of living (see §4.2).

### 4.5 Loan repayment model (illustrative: 12% p.a., monthly EMI)

| Loan | Balance at repayment start | 24-month EMI | 36-month EMI | 60-month EMI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹25 L, interest paid during study | ₹25.0 L | ₹1.18 L | ₹0.83 L | ₹0.56 L |
| ₹25 L, 12 months' interest capitalised | ≈₹28.2 L | ₹1.33 L | ₹0.94 L | ₹0.63 L |
| ₹30 L, interest paid during study | ₹30.0 L | ₹1.41 L | ₹1.00 L | ₹0.67 L |
| ₹30 L, 12 months' interest capitalised | ≈₹33.8 L | ₹1.59 L | ₹1.12 L | ₹0.75 L |

**Post-study cash-flow check (Galway or Limerick, single person, 2026 Irish tax rules, approximate):** A €45,000 salary nets about **€37,000 a year (≈€3,080 a month)** after income tax, USC and PRSI. With a regional single-room budget of about €1,500 a month all-in, the monthly surplus is roughly **€1,500 (≈₹1.65 L)**. That supports a **36-month base-case repayment** with prepayments, and a 24-month payoff only in the upside case. **Plan on 36–60 months and prepay when possible.** Do not build the decision on a 20-month payoff.

**Loan tactics:**
* Get **written** sanction offers from at least three lenders: one public bank (SBI Global Ed-Vantage, which usually needs collateral above ₹7.5 L) and two NBFCs (for example Avanse, HDFC Credila, Auxilo or InCred, which offer unsecured loans with a co-applicant). Compare **effective rate, processing fee, moratorium interest treatment, prepayment penalty and the tuition/living disbursement split**.
* **Pay simple interest during the course** if family cash flow allows. It rises to about ₹30k a month once a ₹30 L loan is fully disbursed, and paying it avoids about **₹3.8 L** of capitalised interest. Even partial payments help.
* Loan-financed education remittances from a recognised lender are **exempt from TCS** under current LRS rules. Confirm with the bank at disbursement. Self-funded remittances above ₹10 L attract TCS.
* Interest paid on the loan qualifies for the **Section 80E** deduction for up to 8 years if anyone in India (Bhumika or a co-borrowing parent) has taxable income.

## 5. Post-study employment framework

| Stage | Permission | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| During study | **Stamp 2** | Up to 20 hrs/week in term; 40 hrs/week 1 June – 30 September and 15 December – 15 January. 2026 minimum wage €14.15/hr. |
| After results | **Stamp 1G (Third Level Graduate Programme)** | Level 9: up to **24 months** (12 + 12, renewable if conditions are met). Full-time work allowed without an employment permit. |
| To stay long term | **Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP)** | €40,904 minimum for roles on the Critical Skills Occupations List with a relevant degree; **€36,848 for a recent relevant graduate** (within 12 months). Leads to Stamp 4 after 2 years. |
| Alternative | **General Employment Permit (GEP)** | €36,605 minimum; **€34,009 for a recent qualifying Irish graduate**. Occupation eligibility applies. |

**Salary bands vs thresholds (Dublin 2026 recruitment guides):**

| Role | 0–3 yrs | 3–5 yrs | Clears GEP? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tax Analyst / Senior (Big 4, in-house) | €45–55k | €50–60k | ✅ Comfortably |
| Risk Analyst | €40–60k | €45–70k | ✅ |
| Regulatory / Financial Reporting Analyst | €40–52k | €50–65k | ✅ |
| Compliance Analyst | €30–35k (pure entry) → €35–45k | €35–45k | 🟡 Target the upper band. Her 3.5 years of prior experience should be pitched as 3–5 years. |

**Strategic implication:** Tax, regulatory reporting and risk analytics clear permit thresholds more reliably than generalist compliance roles. That is why the Galway accounting-analytics route is ranked #1.
