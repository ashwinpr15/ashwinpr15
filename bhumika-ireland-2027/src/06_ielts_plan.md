**Why aim for 7.0 instead of 6.5:** a 6.5 just meets the minimum, so one weak band (usually Writing) means a conditional offer and a retake. A 7.0 removes that risk and looks stronger for the merit scholarships and GOI-IES.

**Which test:** take **IELTS Academic**, *not* General Training. General Training is for migration and is **not accepted** for university admission. "IELTS for UKVI" is not needed for Ireland either.

## 4. Five-week study plan (≈ 65 hours)

| Week | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (19–25 Oct)**: format and baseline | Understand every question type | Watch one "format overview" video per module (§5). Do **Cambridge IELTS Academic Test 1** under timed conditions. Make an error log (a notebook or spreadsheet with the question type, why it was wrong, and the fix). |
| **2 (26 Oct – 1 Nov)**: Reading and Listening technique | Speed and accuracy | Reading: about 20 minutes per passage. Practise True/False/Not Given, Matching Headings and Sentence Completion. Listening: spelling of numbers, dates and names; map/plan labelling. Do 2 sections of each every day. |
| **3 (2–8 Nov)**: Writing | Usually the band that pulls Indian candidates below 7 | **Task 1** (graphs, tables, processes): an overview sentence, key trends and comparisons, no opinions, 150+ words in 20 minutes. Her analyst work makes this natural. **Task 2** (essay): a clear position, 4–5 paragraphs, 260–290 words in 40 minutes. Write **5 Task 2 essays and 4 Task 1 reports**, then compare each with band-7/8 model answers. |
| **4 (9–15 Nov)**: Speaking and full mocks | Fluency and coherence | Record answers on the phone every day: Part 1 (personal questions), Part 2 (a 2-minute talk on a cue card; practise 15 common cue-card topics), Part 3 (discussion). Listen back for fillers, pauses and repeated words. Do **two full mock tests** on the weekend. |
| **5 (16–22 Nov)**: polish and test week | Exam conditions | One full mock at the same time of day as the real test. Light review of the error log only. **No new material in the last 48 hours.** Sleep well the night before. |

**Daily rhythm on weekdays (about 60–75 minutes):** 15 minutes of listening practice on the commute, 30 minutes of reading or writing, 15 minutes of speaking recording, 5 minutes updating the error log.

This is useful for spotting patterns, but AI band estimates can be generous, so treat them as practice feedback, not a prediction.

## 6. Module strategies for a 7.0

**Listening (30 minutes + 2 minutes of checking on the computer test).**

- Read the questions *before* each section plays and underline the answer type (number, name, noun).
- Spelling and plural "s" are marked strictly.
- A 7.0 needs about **30/40**.

**Reading (60 minutes, 3 passages, no extra transfer time).**

- Skim each passage for 2 minutes, then answer the questions in order.
- *Not Given* means the text does not say. Do not infer.
- Never spend more than 20 minutes on one passage. A 7.0 needs about **30/40** on Academic.

**Writing (60 minutes). This is the band most likely to decide the result.**

- **Do Task 2 first** (40 minutes). It carries twice the weight of Task 1.
- **Task 2 structure:** introduction (paraphrase the question + a clear position) → two body paragraphs (one idea each, explanation + example) → conclusion. Avoid memorised phrases and over-complex vocabulary; examiners reward accuracy and clear linking.
- **Task 1:** always include an **overview** of the main trends. Leaving it out caps the score at about 5–6 for Task Achievement.
- On the computer test, watch the word counter: Task 1 at least 150 words, Task 2 at least 250.

**Speaking (11–14 minutes, face-to-face).**

- Answer in 2–3 sentences in Part 1 and extend naturally.
- In Part 2, use the 1 minute of preparation to note 4–5 keywords (not sentences) and speak for the full 2 minutes.
- In Part 3, give opinions with reasons and examples. Her workplace experience is useful material for topics like technology, work and education.
- Fluency and natural pronunciation matter more than a "foreign" accent. It is fine to say "That's an interesting question, let me think…".
